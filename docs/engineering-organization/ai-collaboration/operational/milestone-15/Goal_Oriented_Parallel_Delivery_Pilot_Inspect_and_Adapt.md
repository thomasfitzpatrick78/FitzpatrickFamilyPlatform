## Pilot Outcome

The pilot demonstrated the intended Engineering Organization behavior:

- one outcome remained active while Alpha, Bravo, Charlie, and Architecture Integration advanced under separate authority;
- a blocked lane did not stop independently eligible lanes;
- current repository state overrode conversation assumptions;
- dependencies were brought forward when required by the active outcome;
- shared-path work remained serialized;
- material decisions were reduced to exact response forms;
- evidence and stop conditions drove replanning; and
- Owner feedback changed the operating-model requirements without widening existing workstream authority.

The pilot is not complete. Charlie publication, Alpha correction and adoption, Bravo Gate 2 preparation, passive-capability implementation, governed publication, fresh-session recovery, and later activation decisions remain open.

## What Worked

### Separate authority with parallel flow

Alpha prepared goal-delivery artifacts, Bravo resolved PLAT-15.1A control gaps, and Charlie applied and validated its publication package concurrently. No lane borrowed another lane's authority.

### Same-turn replanning

When Charlie validation regenerated six reports, current evidence was preserved and package options were prepared. When Alpha evidence hashes drifted, only Alpha stopped. When the safety reviewer later required a more explicit report-restoration acknowledgment, Charlie stopped while Alpha, Bravo, and adoption preparation continued.

### Compact human gates

Several independent decisions were accepted in one bounded response while authority gained and not gained remained explicit.

### Repository-first control

The checkout, path inventory, hashes, work packages, validators, and stop conditions controlled execution. Conversation approval did not override byte drift, hygiene findings, safety review, or missing later-gate authority.

## What Did Not Work

### Late dependency checks

A necessary PLAT-15.1A check was originally scheduled later than the active goal required. Dependency timing was not connected to a deterministic latest-safe-check rule.

### Excessive Owner orchestration

The Owner had to provide long prompts, restate context, ask to continue, and coordinate transitions between architecture discussion and repository execution.

### Non-atomic external package sealing

Alpha and Bravo each refined evidence after earlier hashes had entered a decision package. Both were caught before unsafe action, but the workflow lacked a mandatory freeze-and-seal order.

### Validator side effects and Git hygiene

Charlie validators regenerated six tracked reports outside the original six-path package. Two readiness reports contained a personal absolute checkout path. Existing tracked versions contain the same field, but newly staging that content would reaffirm a Git hygiene defect.

### Conversation-dependent continuity

The repository already declares chats to be working sessions and repository artifacts to be durable memory, but it lacks an active goal and snapshot spanning the pilot's workstreams, dependencies, decisions, evidence, and next gate.

### Safety acknowledgment precision

Decision 10 accepted report restoration, yet the patch safety reviewer required a direct statement that current reports would be replaced by older HEAD contents and that external evidence would remain the validation record. Decision-card generation must include the operational risk language needed by the execution surface.

## Adopted Adaptations

### Goal as the coordination unit

Use one durable outcome goal above independently authorized workstreams. Retain one active item per stream and Architecture Integration ownership of shared paths.

### Just-in-time dependency policy

Each dependency carries freshness, need-by time, verification duration, decision allowance, execution margin, latest-safe-check time, evidence, and stop/replan behavior.

### Same-turn eligibility recomputation

Every blocker or invalidation stops the affected lane, recomputes eligibility immediately, and continues all unaffected authorized lanes.

### Atomic package sealing

Every decision-bound external package uses this order:

1. finish all target artifacts;
2. stop all writers;
3. run semantic, path, hygiene, applicability, and test checks;
4. compute target hashes;
5. produce manifest and audit;
6. compute manifest and audit hashes;
7. produce the decision card last;
8. independently recompute every bound hash; and
9. prohibit further writes unless explicitly superseded.

### Validator-output preflight

Before repository application, identify every validator that may modify tracked reports. Pre-authorize inclusion/reconciliation, isolated-only execution, or a stop before mutation. Never improvise report restoration after validation.

### Generated-evidence hygiene

Validators emit sanitized repository identity and repository-relative evidence. Personal names and absolute checkout paths remain outside Git. The readiness validator requires a focused source-and-test correction.

### Repository-governed continuity bundle

Publish the goal definition, current snapshot, authority references, dependency observations, decision ledger, shared-path state, requirement evidence, unresolved decisions, and next gate. Chat or model memory may help locate these records but is not required to reconstruct them.

### Owner attention contract

The Owner supplies outcomes, priority, value judgment, and material decisions. Codex owns routine decomposition, checks, parallel coordination, replanning, evidence, and continuation within authority. ChatGPT supports strategy, architecture, and inspect-and-adapt work but does not become durable authority.

## Pilot Measures

Record sanitized counts and durations for:

- material Owner decisions requested;
- prompts required only to say “continue”;
- time waiting for human decisions;
- late or prematurely checked dependencies;
- blocked lanes with eligible alternatives;
- eligible-lane continuation rate;
- decision-card regeneration caused by evidence drift;
- safety-review stops caused by insufficient risk language;
- chat-history reconstruction events; and
- completion requirements with authoritative evidence.

Do not record personal names, prompts, customer data, credentials, absolute paths, or detailed local reports in Git.

## Exit Criteria

The pilot may be proposed for closeout only after:

1. Charlie's accepted subject and traceability are published with remote equality proved, or a later exact decision changes that outcome.
2. Alpha's passive capability and operating-model adoption are reconciled, governed, implemented, validated, accepted, and published.
3. Bravo reaches the next truthful Gate 2 decision without bypassing acquisition, trust, protected-action, offline-UTM, or supported-Linux gates.
4. The Owner brief and repository-first continuity bundle are governed artifacts.
5. A fresh session proves recovery without prior chat history.
6. Generated repository evidence passes personal-name and absolute-path hygiene checks.
7. A completion audit proves every pilot acceptance requirement.
8. Any operational activation receives separate exact approval.
